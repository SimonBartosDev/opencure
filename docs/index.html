What OpenCure is — and is not

We built a multi-pillar platform, then subjected it to a strict, leakage-controlled evaluation. The honest result reshaped what we claim.

What it is

  • An open, leak-honest evaluation instrument that shows how much apparent repurposing signal is really leakage or popularity.
  • A narrow genetics-anchored prioritization / triage tool for the diseases where human genetics implicates a causal target.
  • A generator of transparent, reproducible hypotheses for expert review.

What it is not

  • Not a validated predictor. There is no trustworthy benchmark accuracy figure; the multi-pillar fused score does not beat a trivial popularity baseline.
  • Not a discovery engine. Where the genetics signal works, it largely rediscovers a disease's existing drug.
  • Not wet-lab validated. Zero predictions have experimental confirmation.

The honest findings

Leak-controlled evaluation against a held-out set, with a trivial popularity (knowledge-graph node-degree) baseline as the comparator. A method earns its place only by beating that baseline.

The 0.997 was leakage

The original ensemble AUC-ROC came from knowledge-graph features trained on the very treats edges used as evaluation labels — the model was graded on recalling its own training graph. Retrained leak-free, it was at chance on a fair temporal test. The number has been withdrawn from all materials.

We measured the leakage: it was ~50 points

Our best-evidenced result (n=960). Holding the candidate pool (24,313), the degree baseline, the rank convention, the scorer and the query relations all fixed, and changing only whether the embeddings saw the held-out treats edges in training: a knowledge-graph model scores Hit@10 52.8% contaminated (median rank 8) and 3.1% retrained leak-free (median 538) — against a popularity baseline of 2.7%. Contamination accounted for 49.7 points. It was not a small-pool artifact: re-measured on the honest pool, the contaminated model still scores 52.8%. This is ~18× more evidence than our one positive result rests on. Scorecard →

No similarity family beats popularity

Knowledge-graph embeddings, chemical-structure similarity (ChemBERTa), and cell-morphology similarity (JUMP Cell Painting) each fail to beat the popularity baseline. To be precise: only morphology is strictly worse (Hit@10 2.8% vs 3.0%); chemistry and the knowledge graph merely tie — nominally a hair higher at Hit@10 (4.4% vs 3.8%) while worse on median rank and Hit@100. None demonstrates a real gain. "Be similar to a disease's known treatments" is an incoherent target — a disease's treatments are mechanistically unalike.

One signal beats the baseline: human genetics

Genetics-anchored target prioritization (disease → human-genetics causal gene → drug with a curated mechanism on that gene) beats popularity ~5× on the genetics-covered subset (Hit@10 20.8% vs 3.8%, median rank 64), leak-free — consistent with the established finding that genetically-supported targets succeed ~2.6× more often in trials.

It adds real lift over popularity, and survives a temporal test

The genetics signal is strongest exactly where popularity is useless (low-degree, non-hub drugs), and it survives a temporal holdout: with genetics frozen at Feb 2020 — provably predating the 2020–2023 test approvals — it still ranks the true drug above popularity on 38 / 40 post-2020 pairs. The honest prospective Hit@10 is a modest ~10% (a contaminated 32.6% deflated once posterior-encoded genetics is removed). Full validation →

But it rediscovers, and covers only part of the space

Of 93 screened diseases, roughly 69 are genetics-covered; pathogen-driven neglected tropical diseases have no human-genetic architecture and are honestly returned as not assessed. And where genetics is strong, drug development usually already happened — so the top lead is typically the existing drug, not a novel one. Across every angle tested, no novel, credible, wet-lab-ready lead was found. Read the full report →

OpenCure is an open-source, mission-locked, nonprofit drug-repurposing project (Apache-2.0). We publish negative results as plainly as positive ones: most of a multi-pillar architecture's apparent power was evaluation leakage, only human genetics carried leak-free signal, and we found no novel wet-lab-ready lead. We release the leak-free instrument so others can hold their own tools to the same standard.
